Project 01 · Brand Audit

THEATER.XYZ

A premium Indian D2C fashion label audit.

01
“Theater.xyz has beautiful scenes. It doesn't yet have one story.”

Overview

A brand audit of Theater.xyz, a premium Indian D2C fashion label — built on publicly available site and social research, distinguishing what was directly observed from what is sourced or inferred.

The Challenge

Visually distinctive brand treated as separate scenes. Theater.xyz has built a genuinely distinctive visual identity — dark-romantic, editorial, cinematic. Product drops like the Kai footwear collection show real skill at turning a single item into a scene with cultural reference points and mood. The question: does that identity actually survive the customer's journey from a campaign image to a checkout page, or does it live only in the moments the brand chooses to art-direct?

The Insight

Tone across the site follows a clear arc — cinematic and evocative at discovery, intimate at brand storytelling, then flat and functional right at the product page and checkout, the exact moment a visitor is deciding whether to buy.

The Strategic Question

What happens when the aesthetic meets a cheaper look-alike? DipYourToes sells near-identical Mary Jane-style footwear at a more accessible price point. If visual identity is the main differentiator, the brand is exposed where pricing depends on being different.

The Strategy

  • Unify the narrative: Extend the cinematic voice into product descriptions and checkout.
  • Reposition the hero: Replace substitutable silhouettes with proprietary designs or multi-category “scenes.”
  • Give navigation a voice: Layer campaign-based “Edits” alongside product categories.

Execution — Proposed Copy Rewrite

Before “A floral woody blend with a touch of spice fun, Tragedy makes for a well-suited evening perfume...”
After “She doesn't ask to be liked. She asks to be remembered. A floral-woody eau de parfum, she arrives uninvited with a sharp, combative flash of lemon, bergamot, pepper and patchouli...” (Composition reframed as The Opening Act, The Interlude, The Final Act).

Project 02 · Campaign Strategy

THEATER.XYZ — “PLOT TWIST”

Dress for the Plot.

02

Overview

An integrated campaign strategy using the Smart Insights Campaign Planning + RACE Framework.

The Problem

Theater doesn't have an awareness problem; it has a wear-frequency problem. Statement pieces are bought predominantly for planned occasions, resulting in high desire but low wear frequency and low repeat purchase rates (common in Indian D2C fashion).

The Insight & Idea

Move statement pieces out of the occasion slot and into the everyday slot (office, coffee runs, commute).

“The twist isn't scheduled. It doesn't wait for Saturday. So the dressing shouldn't either.”

Execution Slate

01Office → TheatreHero Film: Workday transformation
02Coffee Run → Red CarpetEveryday errand with statement jewellery
03Commute → Dressing RoomWeekday transit with statement shoes
04The Twist That Doesn't ResolvePattern-break film

Commercial Model

Merchandised “Scene Bundles” (The Tuesday Scene, The Commute Scene) pairing daily-driver staples with statement pieces to raise AOV by 5–15% and model everyday styling permission.

Project 03 · Brand Creation

MISTEA HOLLOW

A premium tea world built from an unaddressed consumer tension.

03
MisTea Hollow is a brand created from a consumer tension rather than a product — built strategy-first, with the brand world designed to justify the loyalty mechanic, not decorate it.

Consumer Tension — Rational people who still notice small, beautiful things

The target isn't a “wellness” persona. It's someone who lives a deadline-driven, practical life — and privately still notices the light making a rainbow in a glass of tap water, or milk blooming into coffee. That noticing has no outlet in their daily routine, and most tea brands talk to either the clinical “functional” buyer or the aesthetic “cottagecore” buyer, never both at once.

Insight → Opportunity

Whimsy isn't decoration — it's a suppressed habit

Chitra, 29 — deadline-driven, health-conscious, no time to read — is the persona built to hold this tension. On weekends she lights candles, reads, and makes herbal tea; on weekdays that instinct has nowhere to go. The opportunity is a brand that gives her small noticing a ritual and a name, instead of asking her to become a different kind of person to enjoy it.

Brand Permission

“Keep Your Whimsy Alive”

Not an aesthetic tagline, but a permission structure for people who've stopped giving themselves one.

The Pain Point

People know traditional home remedies work — but the effort kills the habit. Soaking ingredients overnight, following a multi-step ritual, staying consistent about it: it's one more mundane chore layered onto an already tiring day, so most people quietly give up on it. MisTea Hollow strips that effort out. The remedy is pre-blended, steep-and-go, and needs no overnight prep — the ritual stays, the labor doesn't.

Positioning → Brand World

The Product System — Peculiar Cures: functional first, story second

Every Cure is built around one genuine, common household remedy — the kind found in an Indian kitchen — tied directly to the specific problem it names, then built into a blend meant to actually taste good rather than read as medicine.

For the Untangling of Thoughts

The Campaign

Keep the Whimsy Alive — a 4-week activation

  • The Potter's Cabinet: limited ceramics made with indie potters, each tied to a specific Cure — collecting all four unlocks a discount.
  • Bookstagram, “Every chapter deserves a cure”: creators pair a current read with the Cure that fits its mood, with monthly featured pairings.
  • Small Rituals: cozy-lifestyle creators fold a Cure into an existing routine — a Weekend Reset, a Slow Morning — rather than treating tea as the whole content idea.

Why It Matters

A brand world exists to make the mechanic believable.

The Cure Seals, Character Cards and Collector's Cup aren't decoration bolted onto a tea subscription — they're what make “keep collecting” feel like continuing a story instead of chasing a discount. That's the strategic bet behind MisTea Hollow: a brand world built specifically to make its own loyalty mechanic make sense, rather than a loyalty mechanic added to a brand world after the fact.

Overview

A complete end-to-end brand creation project built for urban millennials and Gen Z seeking daily, grounding rituals amidst corporate burnout.

The Tension: Daily workplace routines offer caffeinated energy but lack intentional rest or sensory grounding.

The Solution & Brand System: MisTea Hollow — a curated line of premium loose-leaf teas and matcha blends. Built from scratch covering brand positioning, visual identity, packaging design, tone of voice, and digital launch funnel architecture. Packaging is designed to feel intentional rather than disposable — each tin and box doubles as a reusable collectible, built to be kept and displayed rather than thrown away.

Brand World: Whimsical botanical aesthetics, grounded narrative, and functional ritual routines integrated into daily workday pauses.
